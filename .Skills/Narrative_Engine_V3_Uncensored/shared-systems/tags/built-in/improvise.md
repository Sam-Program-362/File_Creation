# Improvise

## Purpose

Authorize proactive creative completion where the concept lacks context, connective information, or necessary supporting elements.

This is a tag-controlled mode, not the engine's default permission to invent freely.

## Required behavior

When applied:
- Identify what the project needs in order to form a coherent, usable whole.
- Fill relevant gaps with fitting details, traits, behaviors, motivations, connective events, supporting characters, institutions, setting details, or other necessary elements.
- Make reasonable creative decisions instead of leaving the project full of unanswered gaps.
- Integrate additions naturally into the appropriate final sections.
- Preserve the original concept, tone, established facts, and Bedrocks.
- Avoid presenting routine completion work as a list of missing elements.

## Missing Elements section

When Improvise is applied, do not include a `Missing Elements` section in the relevant Analysis Sheet. Resolve gaps through the chosen creative development instead.

Do not hide an unresolved major contradiction by pretending it has been solved. A true conflict between explicit facts or Bedrocks still requires resolution.

## Creative boundary

Improvise means **complete the concept faithfully**, not replace the concept.

Do not add major trauma, secrets, relationships, world history, or premise-changing twists merely because they are dramatic. A major addition must serve the user's concept and be logically supported by its tone and direction.

Do not contradict:
- explicit user facts;
- approved project decisions;
- Bedrocks;
- scoped exclusions;
- direct command-local constraints.

## When not applied

Without this tag, the engine may identify important missing information, leave intentional mysteries unresolved, or ask a concise clarification when an unknown would materially change the project.

## Output behavior

Do not label every creative addition as an invention in final project documents. Once accepted as part of the generated result, write it naturally. The Analysis Sheet may distinguish user-provided elements from engine-developed additions where that distinction is useful.
