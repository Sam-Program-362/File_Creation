# Full Physical Description

## Purpose

Produce a genuinely comprehensive physical description of the character's body and visible appearance. This tag does not mean merely describing whether the character is slim, athletic, tall, short, or attractive.

The goal is to describe the character as a complete physical individual, with enough concrete detail that a reader can form a clear mental picture.

## Coverage

Cover all relevant and supportable areas:

### Overall proportions
- Estimated height or height range when the source allows a reasonable estimate.
- Overall frame, build, body type, and proportions.
- Relative proportions between torso, limbs, and overall silhouette.
- General physical presence and how the body is carried.

### Upper body
- Shoulder width and shape.
- Neck and collar area.
- Chest structure or bust where relevant.
- Torso length and structure.
- Waist and abdomen.
- Back, arms, forearms, wrists, hands, and notable details.

### Pelvis and lower body
- Hip width and shape.
- Pelvis and waist-to-hip proportions.
- Buttocks and lower-body silhouette where relevant.
- Thighs, knees, calves, shins, ankles, and feet.
- Leg length and relative lower-body proportions.

### Head and face
- Skull and face shape.
- Forehead, cheekbones, jaw, and chin.
- Eyes, brows, nose, lips, ears, and distinctive facial traits.
- Hair color, texture, density, length, hairstyle, and hairline when visible.
- Skin tone, visible texture, markings, scars, freckles, or other distinguishing traits.

### Physical conduct
- Natural posture and carriage when supported.
- Gait or characteristic movement when shown or established.
- Characteristic body language when it is a stable behavior rather than a temporary pose.
- Physical limitations, distinctive mannerisms, or species-specific movement where relevant.

### Presentation
- Clothing and accessories that are intended as part of the character's stable design.
- Footwear and recurring style.
- Species traits, non-human anatomy, prosthetics, augmentations, or supernatural physical features when relevant.

## Evidence limits

- Do not invent exact measurements from an image.
- If estimating height or proportions, label the result as an estimate and avoid false precision.
- Do not infer exact body measurements from perspective-distorted images.
- Distinguish stable traits from temporary pose, expression, lighting, camera angle, weather, or scene composition.
- Do not describe areas that are not visible or supported as though they were observed. Mark genuine uncertainty or omit the detail.
- If source material does not support a subcategory, do not fabricate it merely to fill the checklist.

## Output behavior

Use clear, concrete prose. Avoid vague one-word labels when the reference supports a richer description. Explain proportions in relation to the body rather than listing disconnected adjectives.

This tag controls physical-description coverage. It does not automatically request a full personality, backstory, or psychological profile.
