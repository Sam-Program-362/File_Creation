# Opening Message Design Procedure

This file defines how to turn established project material into a compelling first playable moment. Read it with `SKILL.md` whenever generating a Scenario.

## 1. What an opening must accomplish

A starting message should create an immediate, understandable situation with enough narrative energy to invite participation. It should not explain the entire project or resolve the interaction before the user can act.

A strong opening normally establishes:

1. **Immediate frame:** where and when the opening occurs, only as specifically as the project supports.
2. **Current activity:** what a relevant character or part of the world is doing at this moment.
3. **Narrative pressure or appeal:** the relationship, task, tension, mystery, atmosphere, event, or dynamic that makes this project interesting.
4. **Response opportunity:** a natural point where the user can influence what happens next.

These are functional components, not mandatory paragraphs or headings. Do not show them as a checklist in the generated message.

## 2. Choose the correct opening focus

Follow the project's narrative gravity:

| Project center | Effective opening focus |
|---|---|
| Relationship-led | A revealing interaction, an unresolved moment, an established routine with tension, or a shift in how two people behave toward each other |
| Character study | A meaningful behavior, contradiction, decision pressure, or revealing situation |
| Slice of life | A grounded activity, social encounter, small event, or daily disruption with room for interaction |
| Mystery | An anomaly, clue, inconsistency, uncertain event, or partial revelation without prematurely giving away the answer |
| Psychological | A visible behavior or external situation that exposes pressure or contradiction while protecting private knowledge boundaries |
| Adventure | A concrete situation, opportunity, obstacle, destination, or decision point |
| Open-world project | A locally specific event that implies a larger accessible world without treating the initial location as the whole setting |
| Open-interaction / ensemble | A focused interaction that leaves room for additional participants without crowding the opening with an unnecessary cast dump |
| Worldbuilding-led RP | A scene that demonstrates an important world feature through lived experience rather than a detached encyclopedia explanation |
| Political or factional | A negotiation, order, public event, competing obligation, or consequence that reveals the active power dynamic |
| Horror or suspense | A credible disturbance, unsettling detail, or mounting uncertainty consistent with the project's actual intensity |
| Fantasy / science fiction | A specific lived situation that naturally reveals relevant speculative rules without an exposition lecture |

Do not force the project into these categories. They are examples of how to apply narrative gravity, not a new fixed classification system.

## 3. Construct a playable situation

### 3.1 Establish what is already happening

Prefer an active moment over a long preamble. A character may be preparing something, waiting, concealing an object, negotiating, repairing, observing, working, avoiding someone, or responding to a current event. The activity should reveal character or project context.

Do not begin every Scenario with a character waking up, entering a room, looking out a window, receiving a message, or noticing the user. Choose a start that belongs to this project.

### 3.2 Put context into the scene

Reveal important context through specific details:
- a custom, tool, uniform, architecture, or social rule in use;
- a character's characteristic phrasing or habit;
- a meaningful object with established relevance;
- a visible consequence of an existing world rule;
- a small exchange that exposes a relationship dynamic;
- a place that feels specific to the world.

Do not stop the opening to explain the history of the object, institution, magic system, or relationship unless the reader genuinely needs that information at once.

### 3.3 Give the scene movement

The opening should change or sharpen something before it ends: a question becomes difficult to avoid, an object is revealed, a character makes an offer, an obligation becomes immediate, a small disruption occurs, a silence takes on meaning, or a choice becomes available.

Movement can be subtle. A quiet interaction does not require an external emergency.

### 3.4 Preserve an open response

The user should have room to decide what their character says or does. The opening can address the user's role, present a circumstance, ask an in-character question, offer an object, or leave a meaningful silence. It cannot answer for the user.

A hook is not necessarily a cliffhanger. It is the point where the situation becomes available for user participation.

## 4. Character voice and interaction

Use only established character behavior and context. A character may be provocative, evasive, formal, vulgar, awkward, detached, theatrical, terse, playful, or emotionally restrained. Do not make every character sound like a narrator who is explaining their feelings clearly.

Subtext can emerge from the mismatch between what a character says and does, but do not invent a hidden motive or secret as fact. If the project defines a contradiction, let it appear through behavior. If it does not, do not manufacture one purely for dramatic interest.

Do not make characters omniscient. A character's dialogue, reaction, or thought must fit what that character knows at this point in the story.

## 5. Scene scale and pacing

Calibrate the opening's scale to the expected scope:
- **Single scene / short RP:** enter close to the central interaction; minimize setup.
- **Medium story:** establish a clear scene and one meaningful pressure point.
- **Longer narrative concept:** establish a strong local opening while allowing the larger premise to unfold later.
- **Open-world setting:** start with one concrete place or activity, but do not imply that the world ends at its boundaries.
- **Ensemble setting:** avoid introducing every cast member at once. Begin with a manageable focus and leave room for the wider cast.

Use the default 2–4 paragraph length from `style-and-format.md`, adjusted to the requested output. Keep prose purposeful. Do not use the opening to summarize future arcs or explain the complete world.

## 6. Distinct alternatives when several are requested

Each alternative should offer a genuinely different entry point into the same project. Possible dimensions of variation include:
- a different immediate activity or event;
- a different established location;
- a different character's opening focus;
- a different emotional temperature within the same canon;
- a different point in the same established situation;
- a different way of revealing the core dynamic;
- a quieter versus more immediate opening, if both match the project.

Do not vary Bedrocks, core facts, or established relationship status between alternatives. Do not introduce a different premise unless the user specifically requests alternate concepts.

For N alternatives, silently plan N distinct entry approaches before drafting. If two approaches would result in essentially the same message, redesign one before writing.

## 7. Sample structural patterns

These are design patterns, not mandatory templates. Do not copy them mechanically.

### Quiet relationship opening

- Begin with an established shared routine or small interruption.
- Use one or two concrete actions to reveal familiarity, discomfort, distance, or change.
- Give the character a line or action that leaves the next response open.

### Mystery opening

- Establish one observable irregularity.
- Let the character respond according to what they actually know.
- Keep the cause or answer uncertain if the project requires mystery.
- End with a clue, question, or consequence available for the user to engage with.

### Adventure opening

- Establish the immediate objective or opportunity.
- Make the relevant obstacle or decision visible.
- Show enough of the world to ground the situation.
- Stop before choosing the user's response.

### Slice-of-life opening

- Start with a tangible everyday activity.
- Let small behavior, dialogue, and social context carry the scene.
- Introduce only as much disruption as the intended tone needs.

### Tension-led opening

- Put the relevant characters or forces in a specific shared situation.
- Use spacing, timing, dialogue, and restrained behavior to show tension.
- Avoid explaining the entire conflict or deciding how the user reacts.

## 8. Common failure patterns

Avoid these patterns:

- **Premise dump:** explaining every character, institution, history, and rule before the scene begins.
- **Generic cinematic opener:** weather, dark streets, mysterious smirks, and dramatic music that have no project-specific purpose.
- **Forced user entrance:** narrating the user's character walking in, sitting down, arriving, or reacting without authorization.
- **Forced user emotion:** assigning fear, attraction, guilt, confusion, anger, or curiosity to the user's character.
- **NPC omniscience:** letting a character know the user's private thoughts, concealed facts, or information the character has not learned.
- **Premature resolution:** completing the argument, confession, mystery, mission, or decision before the user can participate.
- **Unrequested escalation:** turning a quiet or ordinary concept into danger, conflict, romance, or melodrama.
- **False choice:** presenting a supposed choice after the character has already made the decision for the user.
- **Lore invention:** inventing a major faction, law, secret, relationship, or world event to make the opening seem more dramatic.
- **Template repetition:** using the same scene order, gestures, hooks, and sentence rhythms for different projects.
- **Multiple paraphrases:** delivering several versions that differ only in wording.

## 9. Pre-draft planning check

Before writing, answer internally:

- What is this project's narrative gravity?
- What is the smallest scene frame that makes the concept immediately recognizable?
- Which established character behavior will carry the opening?
- What specific context must appear now, and what can wait?
- What is the immediate movement or pressure?
- What can the user meaningfully respond to?
- Which Bedrocks, knowledge boundaries, and tags constrain the scene?
- If multiple alternatives are requested, what makes each one genuinely distinct?

If a proposed opening requires unsupported canon, choose a different entry point.
