# Concept Analysis

## Purpose

Understand what the user is trying to create and why the concept works. This module identifies the project's narrative identity and structural needs. It does not generate the final character, world, Context Sheet or opening scene.

## When to use

Use for every substantive new project analysis. It is the only Phase 6 specialist that is always required by the Analysis Sheet coordinator.

## Procedure

### 1. State the premise neutrally

Summarize the concept in one or two sentences without adding new lore. Include the central subject, situation or mechanism and the intended experience where known. If the user's idea is deliberately abstract, retain its ambiguity rather than forcing a specific interpretation.

### 2. Identify project type and scope

Determine a useful primary type and, only when helpful, secondary types. Examples from V2 include Open World RPG, Story RPG, Sandbox RPG, Slice of Life, Romance, Fantasy, Psychological, Adventure, Mystery, Character Study, Worldbuilding Project and One-Shot Scenario. These are examples, not an exhaustive taxonomy.

Also estimate expected scope from the user's intent: a single opening, short scenario, medium story, one-session RP foundation, multi-session setting or other stated scope. Do not describe the project as long-term merely because it contains extensive lore. The engine's own normal workflow remains one-session creation.

If no conventional type fits, define a concise custom type. Do not stack genres merely to sound precise.

### 3. Identify the concept core

Determine, where supported:
- **Core concept:** the essential premise or defining mechanism.
- **Core appeal:** what makes the idea compelling or distinct.
- **Core emotion:** the emotional experience the project is intended to evoke.
- **Core dynamic:** the relationship, opposition, social mechanism or interaction that produces movement.
- **Core conflict:** the main source of tension, uncertainty or change, if one exists.
- **Core theme:** the larger idea being explored, if one is evident.

Do not invent a conflict for a peaceful or observational concept. A project can be driven by atmosphere, discovery, routine, relationship, simulation or another kind of narrative interest.

### 4. Find narrative gravity

Identify the element that naturally attracts the greatest narrative attention. Ask:
- What would make the user want to return to this premise?
- Which element generates the strongest meaningful interactions?
- What becomes less interesting if it is removed?
- Which elements mainly support that center?

Examples: a relationship concept may be driven by relational change; a mystery by uncertainty and evidence; an exploration concept by discovery and place; a character study by internal contradiction; a worldbuilding concept by the setting's systems. Treat examples as illustrations, not defaults.

### 5. Map the narrative structure

Determine which elements are central, supporting or atmospheric. Use a flexible structure:
- **Primary focus:** the main source of appeal or progression.
- **Secondary focus:** elements that strengthen or complicate it.
- **Tertiary support:** detail that adds immersion without competing for attention.

Describe relationships between elements when they matter. For example, a political system may create the pressure behind a family conflict; a character's limitation may shape the available plot choices.

### 6. Identify the project's operating assumptions

Establish only what is reasonably supported:
- open or closed cast;
- open or bounded setting;
- linear, branching, episodic, emergent or undefined progression;
- player role or persona relevance, if supplied;
- level of realism, stylization or genre convention;
- intended tone and narrative intensity;
- whether the project needs RPG rules or only narrative foundations.

Do not automatically add mechanics because the concept is called an RPG. Do not include a player persona unless supplied or clearly required by the project; the V2 User Persona rule remains a relevant constraint.

### 7. Detect development needs

Separate essential structure from optional enrichment. Identify:
- information required to make the requested output coherent;
- relationships or causal links that need to be made explicit;
- likely overbuilding risks;
- promising development opportunities that preserve the concept;
- unanswered questions that materially affect interpretation.

Do not invent major secrets, trauma, motivations, relationships, history or world events without support or authorization. The Improvise tag changes how missing supporting elements are completed, but does not authorize concept replacement or Bedrock violations.

### 8. Recommend output priorities

Map the concept to likely deliverables without generating them. For example:
- a character-driven premise may prioritize Character Sheet and Context Sheet;
- a setting-driven premise may prioritize World Sheet and Context Sheet;
- a project whose separate narrator needs conditional instructions may benefit from a Lorebook Plan;
- a project intended for public presentation may need a Description;
- a starting scenario is usually most useful after the Context Sheet and relevant assets exist.

These are recommendations, not automatic generation. Respect the user's requested outputs.

## Output to coordinator

Return a concise structured analysis containing:
1. Neutral premise summary.
2. Project type and scope, including uncertainty where relevant.
3. Core concept, appeal, emotion, dynamic, conflict and theme where applicable.
4. Narrative gravity and priority hierarchy.
5. Relevant operating assumptions (cast, setting, progression, mechanics, player role, tone).
6. Development needs and meaningful unknowns.
7. Recommended deliverable priorities.
8. Evidence status for interpretations that could change the user's concept.

The Analysis Sheet coordinator, not this module, decides the final user-facing layout.

## Constraints

- Do not mistake genre labels for a complete concept understanding.
- Do not assume every project needs a central villain, conflict, climax, quest or world map.
- Do not overdevelop tertiary details at the expense of the narrative gravity.
- Do not flatten several distinct concepts into one vague genre.
- Do not silently rewrite the premise to make it more conventional.
- Do not generate final assets here.

## Completion check

- [ ] Premise represented without unsupported additions.
- [ ] Type and scope fit the user's stated intention.
- [ ] Narrative gravity is identified or uncertainty is acknowledged.
- [ ] Primary, supporting and atmospheric elements are distinguished.
- [ ] Development opportunities support, rather than replace, the original idea.
- [ ] Output priorities are recommendations, not forced actions.
